# Iteration: from comparison to evidence audit

The question is: **When two countries receive almost the same overall score, which protections differ—and how well do the legal texts support the comparison?**

The official DWRAP platform already offers field/strand decomposition, country comparisons, trends, distributions, benchmarks, weighting choices, maps and downloads. It also has methodological explanations. A narrated collection of those charts would be insufficiently distinctive. We inspected its retrievable interface, not every runtime interaction, so we make no claim that it lacks all legal excerpts.

Our contribution is the connected task: reproducible matched-total search → largest field gap → recorded code and exact codebook meaning → cited clause → population, date and exceptions → unresolved evidence status where appropriate. Reading the law can complicate the story, not merely decorate it.

## Visual sequence

1. **Paired dot chart:** five shared linear axes support precise comparison. Overall numbers are directly labeled. The largest gap receives restrained emphasis. Switching pairs moves stable marks briefly; reduced-motion preferences remove transitions. This follows the perception and animation material: encode magnitude by position, retain object identity, and use transitions to explain a state change rather than to entertain.
2. **Documents and codes:** a typographic, two-column evidence spread provides a semantic zoom from field to provision. Exact excerpts, source links, code meanings, dates and scope appear together. This connects the class's text-visualization and explanation material without treating a word cloud as legal analysis.
3. **Sortable matrix:** one cell per country-field, with values always visible, reveals profiles across the full 2022 cross-section. Search and sorting change the analytical view; selecting a country links back to its strongest eligible comparison. This applies interaction, overview-to-detail and coordinated-view ideas.
4. **Step timeline:** field selection and year inspection show the pair over time on a stable 0–1 axis. Discrete steps avoid inventing gradual policy change. Color and shape/line style jointly identify countries. No autoplay is needed to understand the result.

The three chart forms are substantially different and interactive beyond tooltips, aligned with the assignment. The reading path borrows Pudding/Upshot practices—one question at a time, direct annotations, clear prose, and progressive disclosure—while keeping the underlying evidence reviewable.

## Why the network is deferred

This dataset contains policy attributes, not links showing migration, influence, treaty relationships or policy diffusion. A similarity network would be a derived construction sensitive to distance and threshold choices. The matrix currently answers the comparison question more transparently and makes a smaller MVP feasible. A future network would need an explicit edge definition, sensitivity checks, stable layout and a demonstrated task that the matrix cannot serve as well.

## Legal cases

Finland–Namibia is the lead because the overall values are close and its largest field difference, movement, has traceable contrasting provisions. We retain a prominent exception: the Finnish general guarantee coexists with individual residence/reporting restrictions. Both countries have the same encampment code. The short quotes illuminate one part of the field without proving all the coding.

Austria–Philippines becomes a second, deliberately different case. EO163 (2022) directs institutions to improve service access but is absent from the supplied title field. This motivates a curator question, not an unreviewed data correction. Chad was not chosen for the lead because apparent code/clause discrepancies need further checking.

## MVP boundary

Two curated cases; all 405 computed near-total pairs; 198 country entities in the 2022 matrix; annual histories for the selected pairs. Legal coverage is explicitly incomplete. Remaining work includes a second human review of each law/version, additional cases, usability testing with classmates, and the assignment video/report. The MVP is not the full final-project submission.

## Chinese synopsis

本轮的重点是把“相近总分、不同分项”深化为可追溯的证据审查：配对规则 → 最大分项差异 → 原始编码 → 法律原文 → 适用人群、版本与例外。已有官方仪表盘已支持分项拆解、国家比较和时间趋势，因此这些图表本身不是创新点。我们的新增价值在于把数值比较与具体条文连接，并允许条文暴露数据覆盖或解释上的问题。

芬兰与纳米比亚作为主案例，展示一般性的迁徙自由保障与特定居住命令下的事先许可要求。同时明确芬兰法律也存在个别限制，不能把一句话解读成无条件自由。奥地利与菲律宾则用于展示数据覆盖疑问：菲律宾2022年的行政命令涉及公共服务，但未出现在提供的数据标题字段中。所有官方分数保持原样，不擅自修正。
